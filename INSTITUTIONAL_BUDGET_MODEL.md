# Institutional Budget & Maintenance Model — v0.64.0

## Design law
Resilience is not free and infrastructure is not permanent. Institutions must collect resources, choose priorities, build projects, and maintain what earlier generations constructed.

## Annual cycle
Each region maintains an institutional treasury. Annual revenue is derived primarily from prosperity, public order, and infrastructure. The region then selects a dominant priority based on current conditions: **Resilience, Commerce, Security, or Welfare**.

## Capital projects
Institutional resilience projects require sufficient treasury capital before construction can begin. Construction still imposes a temporary prosperity opportunity cost and consumes real campaign time. Hero-backed projects remain possible as targeted external support.

## Maintenance
Completed works have condition (0–100) and annual upkeep. Adequately funded assets remain near full condition. Deferred maintenance degrades condition. Crossing poor-condition bands gradually erodes some of the preparedness or infrastructure benefit that project originally created.

## Consequence
A rich region can maintain inherited defenses and invest proactively. A region trapped in long-term poverty may face a downward spiral: weak revenue → deferred maintenance → weaker resilience/infrastructure → larger disaster losses → still weaker revenue. This is intentional but should be balanced with recovery, outside aid, hero intervention, and good-year momentum.
