# Political Agenda, Influence & Leadership Model — v0.66.0

## Core law
Institutions do not reset politically each budget year. They remember wins, losses, allies, scandals, and accumulated leverage.

## Persistent institution state
Each settlement institution tracks:
- satisfaction (-10 to +10)
- influence (0.5 to 3.0+ effective range)
- current agenda priority
- agenda momentum
- last year the agenda advanced
- known faction backers

## Agenda momentum
Repeated budget victories increase momentum. Sustained dissatisfaction or changing regional conditions can weaken or redirect an agenda. Momentum modestly increases effective political weight; it does not replace objective regional pressures.

## Influence drift
Influence changes annually from:
- winning or losing budget priorities
- persistent satisfaction/dissatisfaction
- agenda momentum
- faction backing or hostility
- active institutional scandals

Influence is intentionally slow-moving so a single vote does not remake a region.

## Faction backing
Existing regional factions can reinforce or undermine institutions when their interests plausibly align. This uses already-existing factions only; the political model does not invent factions merely to create backing.

## Leadership eras
A sufficiently dominant institution can become the leading political bloc when its influence clearly exceeds rivals. The region then enters a persistent leadership era aligned with that bloc's agenda (Resilience, Commerce, Security, or Welfare).

Leadership philosophy biases future budget contests but does not dictate them absolutely. Objective crises and competing institutions can still overturn the dominant agenda.

## Scandals
If opposition becomes severely dissatisfied while one bloc dominates, a scandal World Fact can emerge around the dominant institution. Scandals reduce future influence while active and can become quest/political content through the generic World-Fact system.

## Historical principle
Political control should emerge from accumulated history:
repeated wins -> momentum -> influence -> dominance -> policy era -> reactions/scandals -> possible decline or replacement.
