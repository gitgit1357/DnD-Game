# Institutional Politics & Contested Budgets — v0.65.0

## Design law
Regional budgets are political choices, not omniscient optimization. Institutions have different interests, influence, satisfaction, and preferred spending priorities. Scarce public money therefore creates winners, losers, compromises, and sometimes new World Facts.

## Priority families
- Resilience
- Commerce
- Security
- Welfare

Each institution derives a preferred priority from its type, stated interest, and current regional conditions. Military bodies heavily favor security; merchant/trade groups favor commerce; faith institutions tend toward welfare; scholarly bodies tend toward resilience/preservation; civic councils remain more balanced.

## Annual budget contest
At each annual review:
1. Regional conditions contribute objective pressure scores.
2. Institutions cast weighted preference pressure according to influence.
3. The highest combined score becomes the annual priority.
4. Institutions aligned with the winner gain satisfaction; losing institutions lose satisfaction.
5. Close or broadly divided contests can create a persistent `institutionalPolitics` World Fact.

## Contested budget World Facts
A serious budget dispute can enter the normal World-Fact Director and generate a local mediation/investigation contract. Resolving the quest reduces institutional tension, but does not erase the historical vote or the institutions' underlying interests.

## Maintenance politics
Completed assets are maintained in priority order. Under constrained treasuries, politically favored assets are more likely to receive upkeep while lower-priority works may deteriorate. This creates durable consequences from political choices without requiring a separate city-management game.

## Capital investment politics
Autonomous institutional construction still requires treasury capital. Recommended projects are influenced first by actual urgent conditions, then by the region's adopted annual priority. This means political priorities shape what gets built when multiple reasonable investments compete.

## Guardrails
- Institutions do not get infinite budgets.
- Political disagreement does not automatically become a quest; only sufficiently contested decisions become World Facts.
- Hero mediation does not automatically create fame; normal visibility/attribution rules still apply.
- Resolving a dispute does not rewrite institutional interests or past votes.
- Political memory persists through the campaign and can be consumed by future systems.
